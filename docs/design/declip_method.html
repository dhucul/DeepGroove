The readout is the point, not the switch. Sparse costs roughly 700× the peak method, so a user who waits five minutes on one side and seconds on the next deserves to see why. It states the two numbers the choice was actually made from, so the answer is checkable rather than magic. It reads per channel when the two disagree.

Why an override exists at all

Measured head to head on the samples clipping destroyed. Automatic picks the better of the two in 26 of 32 cells; the rows below are where it does not, and they are the reason the switch is worth its space. Sustained material past 40% clipped is the known miss — its envelope barely decays, so no clean frame survives to read the material from.

Open questions for you

1. Three segments or two? Drawn as three. If the override is only ever an escape hatch, a checkbox reading “Choose method automatically” plus one segment pair may be less to explain.
2. Labels. “Sparse” and “Peaks” are the engine's names. “Rebuild” and “Bridge” describe what they do to the audio, which may travel better to someone who has not read the papers.
3. Should the readout warn about cost? It could add “≈4 min” when sparse is chosen over a long side. Against: the operation already has a progress bar and a cancel button, and a time estimate that is wrong is worse than none.
4. Segment width. Three buttons across this card is about 78 px each. “Automatic” fits; anything longer needs the shorter labels from (2) — the rack has been bitten by exactly this, twice.

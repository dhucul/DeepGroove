WaveLab — audio montage

A single-lane clip timeline: clips from any number of source files, with gain, fades and crossfades, assembled into a side and rendered straight to a CD package or a DDP image set. This is not the multitrack rewrite the roadmap declined — there is no mixer, no bus, no send and no automation lane. The engine behind it is built and tested: what needs deciding here is the view. The one thing worth looking at closely is the crossfade readout, because the join is the only place a montage can go wrong and the app now measures rather than assumes it.

The lane. One row of clips on the waveform's own time axis, so the ruler, overview bar and playhead work exactly as they do in the editor. A clip draws its own waveform, carries its name and gain in a header strip, and gets edge grips when selected. Overlaps are drawn as an amber zone rather than as a shape on each clip, because an overlap belongs to the pair, not to either clip — and the label on it is the measured correlation and the law that follows from it, which is the one number that says whether the join is right.

The inspector replaces the effect rack for a montage tab, and edits the selected clip: source, the three positions, gain, and the two free-edge fade shapes. The crossfade panel below is read-only except for its length — the shape comes from the incoming clip and the law is solved from the measurement, so there is nothing there to choose wrongly.

The two sides partly cancel. No pair of fades fixes that, so the law falls back and the montage says so instead.

Why the panel is worth its space. Every other montage picks one crossfade law and lives with the other case being 3 dB wrong. This one solves b = −ρa + √(1 − a²(1 − ρ²)) from the correlation measured over the exact span being summed, which is equal power at ρ=0, equal gain at ρ=1 and exact in between. Measured on the built engine: −9.03 dB before a join, −9.02 through it, −9.03 after, both for unrelated material and for two takes of the same recording. The panel shows the number that produced the curve, so a join that is about to be wrong is visible before it is heard.

Clips are placed at their timeline positions and each source is read once, so a clip used twice is not resampled twice. The peak is reported rather than limited: overlapping clips can sum past full scale, and that is a decision for you, not for the renderer.

CD and DDP fall out for free. Phase 3 already turns a document plus an ordered set of ranges into a sector-aligned package or a DDP image set, and a montage is an ordered set of ranges — so each clip becomes a CD track and the montage renders straight to a plant deliverable. That is the reason the roadmap gated this phase until delivery had shipped.

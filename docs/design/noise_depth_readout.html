Noise & Hiss Reduction — showing the depth the tool actually chose

The reduction depth now follows how much hiss there is to remove, so the slider is a ceiling and the applied value can be far below it — or zero. Without a readout that is a slider whose travel does nothing, which reads as a broken control.

Tighter, and the applied figure sits where the eye already looks for a value. But “— / 10 dB” is a poor way to say “not reducing”, and the value column is shared by every other slider in the dialog, so this one alone would carry two numbers.

Why the card has to say anything at all. Measured over 108 corpus cells, a fixed 10 dB reduction scores −0.85 dB segmental — worse than leaving the audio alone — because a fixed depth applied to hiss already 30 dB under the programme costs more music than it saves noise. Scaling the depth takes cells that come out worse than doing nothing from 46 of 108 to 15, and cells worse than −3 dB from 31 to 8. The tool is now right to ignore most of the slider on a clean transfer, which is exactly why it must be seen to be doing it.

The slider keeps its label and its range. It already says Maximum reduction, so it was always a ceiling; nothing about the control changes and no preset is invalidated. Only the readout is added.

Recommendation: A. It matches the pattern already shipped for declip, it states the measured number rather than a verdict, it has room for the longest wording with 97 px to spare, and it degrades to an ellipsis rather than a clipped glyph if the card is ever squeezed. B is worth having only if the second line proves too noisy in use.
